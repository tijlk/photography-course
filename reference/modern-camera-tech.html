Modern camera tech

What changed after the 30D, and what to check when buying.

Electronic viewfinder (EVF)

  • Mirrorless: no mirror. The lens always shines light onto the sensor, and you view the sensor's picture on a screen or in the EVF.1
  • It shows a preview of how the photo will look: exposure, exposure compensation and white balance.24
  • Exposure simulation (Canon's name) must be enabled for the viewfinder brightness to follow your settings. When it's disabled, the view stays at standard brightness.3 Turn it off for flash in dark rooms.4
  • Limits: possible lag, especially with long exposures, and less dynamic range than an optical finder.2

In-body image stabilisation (IBIS)

  • Moves the sensor to counter camera shake, so it works with lenses that have no stabilisation of their own.5
  • Handheld rule: slowest speed ≈ 1/(35mm-equivalent focal length). Each stop of stabilisation doubles that time. Stabilisation typically gives 2 to 5.5 stops.5
  • Ratings come from the CIPA DC-011 test standard (revised 2024): a lab figure.6
  • Doesn't freeze a moving subject.5 For wildlife and kids, use a fast shutter speed.

Subject-detection autofocus

  • 30D baseline: 9 AF points; One-Shot / AI Servo.7
  • Modern example (Canon EOS R6): Face + Tracking finds and follows faces of people or animals. Subject to detect: People / Animals / Vehicles, where "animals" means dogs, cats and birds. Eye detection.8
  • Servo AF for moving subjects, One-Shot for still ones.9
  • Struggles with: low light, low contrast, night scenes and points of light. Focus manually on stars.8

High ISO

  • 30D: ISO 100–3200.7 R6: ISO 100–102400, expandable to H (204800), which is noisier with lower resolution.10
  • Noise is set mostly by the total light the sensor collects. At the same f-number, a larger sensor collects more.11
  • Full frame vs 1.6× APS-C: 1.6² ≈ 2.6× the area ≈ 1⅓ stops less noise at the same settings.11
  • The top ISO on the spec sheet says little. Compare test images at ISO 3200–6400.

Other changes since 2013

  • DSLRs are discontinued at most brands. Each brand has a new mirrorless mount; Canon RF (2018) takes EF and EF-S lenses through adapters, and EF-S lenses crop 1.6× on full-frame bodies.12
  • Electronic shutter: silent, faster bursts, but fast subjects may distort, and long exposures are limited (R6: nothing slower than 0.5 s).13
  • Battery life is shorter than in DSLRs; some cameras charge over USB.1
  • Cards: SD (UHS-I and UHS-II) or CFexpress, not CompactFlash. Two slots can record every shot to both cards.14
  • Phone app over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi: transfer, remote control, GPS tagging. Built-in interval timer and focus bracketing. Full detail in lesson 0004.

Buying checklist

  1. System: a mirrorless mount with the lenses you'll want, at prices you accept.
  2. Sensor size vs the EF-S 17-55: APS-C keeps it fully useful; on full frame it crops.
  3. Subject detection that covers expected wildlife, plus eye detection.
  4. IBIS and its CIPA rating (body alone or with a lens?).
  5. High-ISO test images at ISO 3200–6400.
  6. Battery: shots per charge, USB charging, cost of a spare.
  7. Card type and cost.
  8. EVF with exposure simulation; a phone app that works. Ignore video specs.
  9. Nice to have: two card slots with "record to both". Several unwiped cards already limit the risk.
