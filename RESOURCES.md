# Photography Resources

## Knowledge

- [Cambridge in Colour: Digital Photography Tutorials](https://www.cambridgeincolour.com/tutorials.htm)
  Long-standing, clearly illustrated tutorials on exposure, metering, depth of field, histograms and sensors. Use for: readable refreshers on fundamentals. (Couldn't be downloaded from the cloud session, so not used for exact figures.)
- [Wikipedia: Exposure value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposure_value), [F-number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-number), [Depth of field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_field), [Shutter speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shutter_speed), [Crop factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_factor)
  Well-referenced definitions with formulas. Use for: exact definitions (stop, f-stop sequence, DoF factors, handheld rule, crop maths).
- [Wikipedia: Middle gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_gray), [Exposure compensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposure_compensation), [Image histogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_histogram), [Color temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_temperature), [Raw image format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_image_format), [Sunny 16 rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunny_16_rule)
  Use for: metering assumptions, histogram reading, kelvin values of light sources, RAW vs JPEG.
- [Nikon USA Learn & Explore: Understanding ISO Sensitivity](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/understanding-iso-sensitivity) and [Understanding Shutter Speed](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/understanding-shutter-speed)
  Manufacturer tutorials. Use for: ISO/noise trade-off and shutter speed vs motion.
- [Canon Camera Museum: EOS 30D](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/dslr792.html)
  Canon's own record of the user's old camera. Use for: the 2006 baseline when comparing with modern cameras.
- [Wikipedia: Canon EF-S 17–55mm lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_EF-S_17%E2%80%9355mm_lens)
  Use for: the lens the user still owns (27–88mm equivalent on APS-C).
