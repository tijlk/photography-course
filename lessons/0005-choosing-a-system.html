Photography course · Lesson 0005

Choosing a system

APS-C with your 17-55, or full frame with new lenses? Then build two kits and compare.

About 20 minutes Phase 3 of 5 · Choosing a camera Uses the checklist from lesson 0004

Your constraints: a fixed budget, no attachment to the 17-55 unless it pays its way, and at most two lenses on a hike. Those turn the brand question into a simpler one first: APS-C or full frame?

1 · The fork in the road

Path A: APS-C, keep the 17-55

How
A Canon APS-C RF body plus Canon's EF-EOS R adapter. Your 17-55 behaves as on the 30D, about 27–88mm equivalent.1
Plus
You already own the standard zoom, so most of the budget goes to the body and a telephoto. A telephoto also reaches further on APS-C: the RF 100-400 becomes about 160–640mm equivalent, which is great for wildlife.
Minus
About 1⅓ stops more noise than full frame at the same settings,2 which shows most in star photos. The 17-55 is a 645 g lens from 2006,3 and it needs an adapter.

Path B: full frame, new lenses

How
A full-frame body plus a new standard zoom and a telephoto. You'd sell the 17-55, because on full frame it would crop the sensor to APS-C.1
Plus
Cleaner high ISO for stars and dusk wildlife, and the best choice of lenses later.
Minus
You pay for a standard zoom as well. And without the 17-55 tying you to Canon, brand is wide open again, so compare systems on their merits (section 2).
The honest summary

The 17-55 is the only rational reason for Canon, and only on path A. On path B, choose the brand for its bodies and lenses, not out of habit.

Things the table can't show:

  • Subject detection: the α7 V lists human, animal, bird, insect and vehicles.9 The Z6III lists faces, birds and animals,8 and the R7 human, animal and vehicle.4 Check each camera's manual to see whether "animal" covers elk and bears.
  • Pre-capture, which saves the moment before you fully pressed the shutter, is listed for the α7 V and Z6III.98 It's very handy for birds taking off.
  • Third-party lenses: Sony published the E-mount specifications in 2011 so other makers can build lenses for it.11 Nikon licenses the Z mount to Sigma, Tamron and Voigtländer.12 On Canon RF, third-party autofocus lenses are limited to APS-C.1 Over the years, that can mean cheaper lenses on Sony and Nikon.
  • R8 Mark II: it adds in-body stabilisation that the R8 lacked, and it's the lightest full-frame option here. But it has a single card slot, so no automatic backup to a second card, and about 220 shots per battery charge with the viewfinder (CIPA).15 On your trip that means more spare batteries, and swapping cards carefully.
