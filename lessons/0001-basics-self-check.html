Photography course · Lesson 0001

Basics self-check

What's still in your fingers after twelve years away?

About 10 minutes Phase 1 of 5 · Fundamentals Answer from memory, no peeking

You shot a 30D for a decade. Most of what you knew still holds: light, lenses and exposure maths haven't changed since 2013. What has changed is the camera around them, which is lesson 0002. Before we get there, this check shows which fundamentals are solid and which have faded, so later lessons skip what you know and cover what you don't.

Every question ties back to the trip: bright peaks, wildlife at the edge of the light, family at camp, stars after dark.

How to take it

Answer each question from memory before you look anything up. Struggling to recall is what makes knowledge stick, and a wrong answer now is useful data, not a failure. Each question locks after one click and shows why, with a source.

Sources

  1. Wikipedia: F-number: standard f-stop scale; each step is one stop.
  2. Wikipedia: Exposure value: one stop = half or double the exposure.
  3. Nikon USA: Understanding ISO Sensitivity: doubling ISO halves the light needed; higher ISO adds noise.
  4. Wikipedia: Depth of field: factors affecting DoF.
  5. Wikipedia: Shutter speed: handheld rule of thumb (shutter speed ≈ focal length on 35mm).
  6. Wikipedia: Crop factor: multiplying focal length by the crop factor gives the 35mm-equivalent field of view.
  7. Wikipedia: Canon EOS 30D: 1.6 angle-of-view crop.
  8. Wikipedia: Middle gray: 18% reflectance; meters assume an average scene.
  9. Wikipedia: Exposure compensation: positive compensation; snowy mountains at +2 EV example.
  10. Wikipedia: Image histogram: left = dark, right = light.
  11. Wikipedia: Color temperature: table of light sources in kelvin; white balance presets.
  12. Wikipedia: Raw image format: settings saved but processing deferred; 12- or 14-bit data.
  13. Wikipedia: Sunny 16 rule: f/16 at 1/ISO in direct sun.
